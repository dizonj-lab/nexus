The problem

Architecture is reviewed on paper, and tested in production.

Standards live in documents

Approved technology, principles and security policy sit in PDFs and wikis. Violations surface late, in the review board, or not at all.

Failure is guessed

Capacity and outage behaviour are estimated in spreadsheets. The real bottleneck, and what a zone loss does, is found by customers.

Decisions lose their reasons

Diagrams, cost models and decisions live in different tools. Months later, nobody can say which version was approved, or why.

Experience is the queue

Resilient hybrid and active-active designs need senior architects. Junior architects lack the context, so everything waits for the few who have it.

The business imperative

Resilience is a board-level question now.

  • Downtime is lost revenue and lost trust. Customers expect services to stay up through a data-centre or cloud-zone failure.
  • Regulators expect resilience to be demonstrated, with tested recovery objectives and traceable decisions, not assurances.
  • Hybrid and multi-cloud multiply the failure modes that one architect has to keep in their head.
  • Change has to be faster and safer at the same time. The review board cannot be the bottleneck.

What NEXUS is built to deliver

  1. Designs that comply with your standards from the first draft
  2. Bottlenecks and single points of failure found before build
  3. Shorter review cycles, with the evidence attached
  4. Junior architects designing safely inside the guardrails

How it works

Design, break, correct, decide. In minutes, not quarters.

  1. DescribeA rough brief in plain language. NEXUS asks only what it needs to know.
  2. DraftA local language model proposes a design. Rules fill the blanks from your ground truth.
  3. CheckPolicy as code: approved stack, EA principles, security, Well-Architected pillars.
  4. BreakLoad to 1,000×, zone and region outages, failover. The simulator names what fails first.
  5. CorrectAgents propose fixes, or you make them. Every option is dry-run with the same rules.
  6. DecideAn architect approves. The decision, its numbers and its reasons are recorded.

Break it fast

Chaos engineering on the drawing board.

Run the experiments a game day would, before anything is built. Change the workload, take out a zone or a region, swap the database, and see the bottleneck, the error rate, the recovery and the cost change as you go.

Load

Push to 1,000×

Requests per second, payload size, read/write ratio and cache hit rate. Autoscaling, rate limits and the first component to break.

Zone

Lose an availability zone

See whether the survivors have the capacity to carry the load, and how long the database takes to fail over.

Region

Lose a region

Active-active or active-passive, DNS failover and health checks, cross-region latency and the data you could lose.

Pattern

Compare resilience patterns

Data centre active-passive and active-active, hybrid, zone-resilient and region-pair designs, side by side.

Data

Choose the database with eyes open

Replicas, global databases and distributed SQL, with their constraints and trade-offs before the decision.

Hybrid

Break the link to the data centre

Data centre and cloud together: Direct Connect or ExpressRoute, internal users on the company network, and what still works when one side is gone.

Figures in the walkthrough above are simulated. The simulator uses deterministic capacity and queueing models: fast, repeatable estimates for comparing options. It complements real load tests and game days; it does not replace them.

Human in the loop

AI assists. Architects decide.

Rules decide, models explain

Risk, cost, capacity and compliance come from policy as code and the simulator. Language models explain them and propose options; they never set the numbers.

Two opinions on every risky change

The Action Guard shows the change as asked, and an independent second opinion, each with before-and-after diagrams, scores and Well-Architected citations.

Agents propose, people apply

Agents work inside a harness: only the tools your role allows, a budget, and a trace of every step. They can propose a changeset; only an architect can apply it.

Every key decision is recorded

Architecture decision records, a versioned change ledger and an audit trail: who decided what, when, why, and with which numbers.
